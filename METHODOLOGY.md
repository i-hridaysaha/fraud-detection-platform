# Methodology

The README holds the proof: the headline numbers, that the pipeline runs, and how it is built at
a glance. The case study holds the narrative. This document holds the detail the README cannot:
what the data looks like, why each column was kept or dropped, how each encoding was chosen, what
every feature computes, and how the serving, monitoring and lifecycle layers were measured. Each
section names the stage document and the artifact it condenses; the stage documents under
`docs/` carry the full analysis, and the 41 decision records are indexed in
[docs/adr/README.md](docs/adr/README.md).

Every number here is read from a committed artifact under `reports/`, regenerated by
`make reproduce`, and [docs/verification.md](docs/verification.md) lists each one with its
source. Values are on the training split unless a split is named.

## 1. Data, entity and split

Stage 0 and 1. `docs/notes/stage-00.md`, `docs/notes/stage-01.md`; `reports/audit.json`,
`reports/split_summary.json`, `reports/memory_profile.json`; ADRs 0001 to 0006.

**The file.** 590,540 transactions, 394 columns, 20,663 fraud (rate 0.03499), over 181.999
relative days of `TransactionDT`. The identity table holds 144,233 rows and 41 columns and joins
on `TransactionID` for 0.2442 of transactions: 0.5477 of fraud rows and 0.2332 of legitimate
ones. `TransactionDT` is an integer offset whose origin is not established; a day is an 86,400
unit block, established by folding the column into that cycle and recovering a diurnal shape with
a peak to trough ratio of 16.99.

**The entity.** There is no customer identifier. Three candidate keys were measured on label
purity, the share of multi-transaction groups that are entirely fraud or entirely legitimate:

| Candidate | Entities | Median transactions | Singleton share | Label purity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `card1` | 13,553 | 4.0 | 0.2541 | 0.8480 |
| `card1 + addr1` | 39,974 | 2.0 | 0.3941 | 0.8899 |
| `card1 + addr1 + floor(TransactionDT / 86400 - D1)` | 217,850 | 1.0 | 0.5748 | 0.9664 |

The three-part key is the entity (ADR 0001). `card1` is not a card: only 0.1539 of `card1`
values with two or more non-null start days have a single one, and the median spread between a
`card1` value's earliest and latest implied start day is 155.0 days. The construction is the
published one (see the README's prior work); the choice was made on purity measured here.

**The split.** Chronological at the 0.70 and 0.85 quantiles of `TransactionDT`, fixed as
integers in `config.py` (ADR 0002):

| Split | Rows | Days | Fraud rate | Entities | Entities unseen in train | Rows on them | Fraud rate, unseen | Fraud rate, seen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| train | 413,378 | 1 to 120 | 0.03517 | 164,702 | | | | |
| val | 88,581 | 120 to 152 | 0.03434 | 46,884 | 0.5736 | 0.5311 | 0.0443 | 0.0231 |
| test | 88,581 | 152 to 182 | 0.03480 | 45,348 | 0.6666 | 0.6598 | 0.0440 | 0.0170 |

Two thirds of the test window's entities are new, and the new ones carry two and a half times the
fraud rate of the ones the training window saw. That is the population structure every later
result sits on.

**Typing.** 18 integer columns take the narrowest type their range fits, 31 string columns become
categories, `TransactionAmt` stays float64 (a float32 round trip moves it by 0.000375 on a
maximum of 31,937.391), and the remaining 384 numeric columns become float32. The typed frame is
0.459 of the naive one (ADR 0005). `assert_train_only` raises on any frame with a row at or past
the training boundary, on an empty frame, and on a null timestamp; `python -O` cannot strip it
(ADR 0006).

## 2. Exploratory analysis

Stage 2. `docs/eda.md`; `reports/eda/*.json`; ADRs 0007 to 0010. Every section is computed on
the training split, and three places that see later rows say so.

**Target.** The training fraud rate is not stationary: over 18 weeks the Mann-Kendall tau is
0.399 at p 0.0214, the weekly rate runs from 0.0207 to 0.0507, a ratio of 2.45. `ProductCD` W
runs at 0.02078 on 297,911 rows and C at 0.11175 on 50,006. Hour position runs from 0.0217 to
0.1001, with the highest rates on the thinnest hours.

**Univariate.** 435 columns profiled. Over the 401 numeric feature columns the median absolute
skew is 10.727 and 359 exceed 1; 30 are effectively constant (one value on over 0.99 of non-null
rows), 229 are zero-inflated. `TransactionAmt` has skew 17.639 raw and 0.499 under log1p; its
cents fraction sits at .00 on 0.5255 of rows and at .95 on 0.2712, and why is not established.

**Missingness.** 372 of 435 columns carry nulls in 67 distinct masks, 24 of them shared by more
than one column and covering 392 columns. The identity table is not one block: its 40 columns
take 26 masks. 251 of the 372 nullable columns are label-linked at a gap of at least 0.010 in
fraud rate between missing and present, in both directions: D7 runs 0.0274 missing against
0.1478 present, `addr1` 0.1125 missing against 0.0251 present. This is the measurement behind
leaving nulls as nulls (ADR 0012).

**Correlation.** Spearman over all 80,200 pairs of the 401 numeric columns, screened in chunks
and recomputed exactly for the 1,762 pairs above 0.80 (ADR 0008). At 0.95 the V block's 339
columns in 14 missingness blocks reduce to 207 representatives. Six pairs above 0.95 involve no V
column, of which C8 with C10 at 0.9710 on all 413,378 rows is the only one measured on every row.

**Bivariate.** Information value over 432 features, the missing bin's share separated: `addr1`
totals 0.4257 of which 0.3260 is the missing bin, so it is a missingness feature. Four columns
with no nulls lead on value alone: C4 0.563, C8 0.553, C10 0.523, `ProductCD` 0.516.

**Temporal.** PSI against test has median 0.0207 with 22 features above 0.25, led by
`card_start_day` at 1.841 (derived from the clock) and `id_31` at 1.500 (19 levels test has that
train never saw). The time-consistency screen (ADR 0009) fits one LightGBM model per column on
days 1 to 30 and scores it on days 91 to 120: 59 columns rank the classes better than chance
early and worse than chance late, `id_38` the strongest at 0.6037 against 0.3634. The 59 flagged
columns and the 22 drifting ones do not intersect.

**Entities.** 164,702 entities over the training rows, median 1.0 transactions, 0.5850
singletons; of the 68,359 multi-transaction entities 0.9675 are label-pure. The median gap
between consecutive transactions is 3,177 seconds on entities that ever carry fraud against
165,394 on entities that never do, which is what the feature windows were set against.

**Quality.** No exact duplicate rows; 5 groups identical up to `TransactionID` and
`TransactionDT`. Agreement between purchaser and recipient email domain carries more than either
domain: 0.09051 agreeing, 0.02689 disagreeing, 0.02011 recipient missing.

**What a train-only rule costs.** `reports/eda/train_only.json` measures the quantile shift and
the unseen-level counts an all-data analysis would have absorbed; the rule is ADR 0007.

## 3. The column plan

Stage 3. `docs/prep.md`; `reports/column_plan.json`; ADR 0011.

Four drop rules in a fixed order, each citing the stage 2 artifact, field, threshold and measured
value it read. 434 raw columns, 288 kept, 146 dropped.

| Rule | Threshold | Dropped | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| effectively constant | one value on over 0.99 of non-null rows, unless information value is at least 0.02 | 14 | 16 of stage 2's 30 flagged columns are rescued on value; `addr2` at 0.9901 one value carries 0.4200 |
| missing and not MNAR | missing rate at least 0.95 and not label-linked | 0 | all nine columns above 0.95 missing are label-linked; the rule would take 51 columns at 0.80 |
| time inconsistent | flagged by ADR 0009 and validation AUC below 0.50 when fitted on the whole training window | 0 | all 59 score at least 0.50 on validation; `id_38` 0.6704 |
| redundant | absolute Spearman at least 0.95, measured on every row for pairs outside the V block | 132 | 88 groups; C10 dropped in favour of C8 |

Two of the four rules drop nothing, and that is the result: the screens of stage 2 were
candidate lists, and the validation probe stage 3 added did not confirm the time-inconsistent
candidates.

## 4. Transformations and missing values

Stage 3. `reports/prep/transforms.json`, `reports/prep/missing_policy.json`; ADRs 0012, 0013.

- **D-column normalisation, rejected.** Subtracting the day index from each D column raises PSI
  on all 15 (D3 from 0.0352 to 2.2125) and lowers single-feature validation AUC on 12 of 15. It
  is applied to no column (ADR 0013).
- **Amount:** log1p and the cents fraction as separate columns; clipping at the 0.999 train
  quantile for the complete-data path, because 0.99 would touch 0.6881 of fraud rows against
  0.0513. Trees get no clipping.
- **Clock:** four relative columns (hour and day-of-week position, day index, seconds into the
  day), never the absolute offset.
- **Missing values stay missing.** Six of eight estimators accept a null and all six learn a
  direction for it, measured by fitting each on a column that is noise where present and null
  exactly on the positive rows: lightgbm 4.7.0, xgboost 3.4.1 and scikit-learn 1.9.0's
  histogram gradient boosting, random forest, extra trees and decision tree score AUC 1.0. The
  two that raise get a median-and-sentinel imputer on the complete-data path. 66 block
  indicators cover 244 kept columns, 23 of them spanning 201 columns; the identity join gets its
  own indicator from `id_01` (ADR 0012).

## 5. Encoding

Stage 3. `reports/encoding_spec.json`, `reports/prep/v_reduction.json`, `reports/prep/schema.json`;
ADRs 0014 to 0017.

**Frequency encoding** on identifiers with at least 50 levels, counts fitted on train.

**Lagged target encoding.** A row's encoding at day d reads only labels of rows at or before
d minus the lag, through cumulative counts per level per day, so a 164,702-level column costs one
array the length of the split. The lag is chosen on the train-minus-validation AUC gap, not on
validation AUC, because a longer lag can only remove information and a validation criterion
would pick lag 0: the gap falls from 0.08117 at lag 0 to -0.00681 at lag 28, lags 14 and 28 are
indistinguishable from zero, and the smaller ships. Smoothing 20 is the interior maximum at that
lag and the sweep records `resolved: false` (ADR 0014). Eighteen columns are encoded.

**The entity key is refused as an encoding input.** Its lagged encoding scores 0.7835 on
validation, which splits into 0.9877 on the 41,536 rows whose entity the training window saw
and 0.4915 on the 47,045 it did not: on seen entities it is the label, read back. The refusal is
a raise inside `fit_target_encoding` (ADR 0015).

**Vocabulary.** Levels below the rare-tail share collapse to one token, and three tokens stay
apart: rare, unseen and missing. `DeviceInfo` goes from 1,546 levels to 281 by structural
normalisation (no brand table) and to 64 after the collapse; `id_31` from 108 to 43 to 11. On
test the rows carrying a level training never saw fall from 500 to 30 and from 8,068 to 149.

**The V block.** Four strategies probed with a small LightGBM model over three seeds:

| Strategy | Columns | Validation AUC | Across three seeds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| all | 327 | 0.8406 | 0.8372 to 0.8414 |
| representative | 196 | 0.8378 | 0.8362 to 0.8414 |
| pca | 122 | 0.8466 | 0.8440 to 0.8466 |
| block_mean | 14 | 0.8375 | 0.8354 to 0.8380 |

The four span 0.0090 and one strategy spans 0.0051 across seeds, so the probe does not separate
them; the tie-break, fewest columns inside the band, ships the block mean. PCA scored highest on
every seed and the artifact records that a different tie-break would have taken it (ADR 0016).

**The contract.** 247 prepared columns, 92 raw survivors and 155 derived; 89 integer, 93 float,
65 categorical; all three splits validate with zero violations, and a `needs_data` test refits
from the CSVs and reproduces the frame's SHA-256 (ADR 0017).

## 6. Feature definitions

Stage 4. `docs/features.md`; `reports/feature_summary.json`, `reports/features/*.json`; ADRs
0018 to 0023.

**The rule.** Every feature for a transaction at time t reads rows strictly before t, on the
whole stream, because that is what an authorisation endpoint has. Same-timestamp rows are
excluded. There is no fitted object beyond the `dist1` bucket edges, so there is nothing for a
train-only guard to check; `tests/test_causality.py` is the enforcement, comparing every feature
against a brute-force recomputation over `TransactionDT < t`, asserting truncation invariance
bit for bit, and asserting the tie exclusion. Truncation invariance changed the implementation:
a global cumulative sum carried rounding from later rows into earlier ones, so window sums
restart per key and running moments use Welford (ADR 0018).

**Three grains.** The entity key; the card (`card1`), because `addr1` is a component of the
entity key and an entity-grain address deviation is identically zero; and the content key, the
six fields that define an identical purchase. Windows are 600, 3,600 and 86,400 seconds,
bracketing the inter-transaction gaps of section 2.

**Own velocity against the native C block.** Spearman across all 84 pairs on train, largest
0.2773, median 0.0658, none at or above 0.50: the two are not the same measurement. The
strongest native counter separates the label about 1.8 times as sharply as the strongest built
velocity (C7 positive against zero 4.525, `vel_count_24h_entity` 2.556). Whether the C block is
a better feature or is not point-in-time cannot be separated here, because its construction is
not published in anything fetched (ADR 0021).

**The duplicate reversal.** The stage 2 grouping (fraud rate 0.04157 inside repeat groups
against 0.02917 outside) is not a feature: it reads the group's later rows. The causal version,
`dup_prior_count`, lifts fraud on train (1.333) and reverses on val (0.814) and test (0.883) with
disjoint intervals on each. Restricted to an hour it holds on all three (2.865, 2.410, 2.019).
The signal is that a purchase repeats soon, not that it repeats. The ship rule became: same sign
on all three splits with disjoint intervals on each (ADR 0022). `dup_seconds_since_prev` and
`dup_prior_count_1h` ship; `dup_prior_count` is a candidate.

**Coverage.** 0.3402 of test rows sit on an entity the training window saw, against 0.7036 with
an earlier transaction anywhere in the stream: a point-in-time feature reaches twice the rows a
train-fitted statistic does. The unbounded `rec_prior_count` degrades out of sample (1.558,
1.134, 1.084 across the splits) and the windowed `vel_count_24h_entity` does not (2.556, 2.625,
2.482).

**Not built.** The published full-dataset aggregation over the identifier (ADR 0020) and any
group statistic that reads a group's later rows; `NOT_BUILT` in the module records the absence
and a test asserts no public name contains `uid` or `agg`. The `D1` origin as a tenure feature
carries PSI 1.8304 against test and is behind a switch that is off.

**Graph features.** Stage 5, `docs/graph.md`, `reports/graph_summary.json`, ADRs 0024 and 0025.
A union-find over `card1`, `addr1`, `P_emaildomain`, `R_emaildomain`, normalised `DeviceInfo`
and `dist1`, grown one timestamp block at a time with labels read only after the 14-day lag. The
end-of-train graph is one component holding 0.9999 of transactions; excluding hub values goes
from one blob to dust with no middle. Component size correlates with `TransactionDT` at 0.9768,
so it is a clock. Four degree features are computed and none passes the ADR 0022 rule; the
component fraud rate is the lagged training rate. A whole-file computation would have handed
the model a leave-one-out label: on the hub-excluded graph, AUC 0.8462 transductive against
0.7087 causal on validation.

## 7. Modelling and evaluation

Stage 6. `docs/training.md`; `reports/model_comparison.json`, `reports/ablations.json`,
`reports/hyperparameter_search.json`, `reports/metric_variance.json`,
`reports/operating_points.json`, `reports/threshold_curve.json`, `reports/shap/`; ADRs 0026 to
0030. The README carries the comparison table and the shipped model's intervals.

- **Metric.** PR-AUC as average precision, read beside ROC-AUC and card-level PR-AUC. On
  validation, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC order five of the 36 model pairs the other way round, and every
  tuned operating point sits at a false-positive rate below 0.034 (ADR 0026).
- **Imbalance.** None, class weight and an in-repo SMOTE, per family. None wins every family on
  validation (ADR 0027).
- **Ablations.** XGBoost, three seeds, twelve stacks, each against a reference differing by one
  block, paired bootstrap on validation. A block this repo builds ships only when it raises
  validation PR-AUC on every seed with the interval excluding zero; a provided block is retired
  only when removing it lowers validation PR-AUC on no seed with the interval excluding zero.
  The second rule was rewritten after the numbers were read, and the artifact keeps both
  (ADR 0028). Removing the native C block costs -0.0590, -0.0663, -0.0658; the M block goes.
- **Search.** Twenty random draws plus the default on three expanding-window folds. The best
  fold mean is 0.5754 against the default's 0.5724; on the full train fit the best scores 0.6048
  on validation against 0.6184, so the default ships.
- **Variance.** 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples by row and by entity; the widest paired
  half-width across the pairs is 0.0172 by row and 0.0592 by card, and the larger is the noise
  band the promotion margin is derived from.
- **Thresholds and bands.** Largest F1 on validation gives 0.2519. The cost matrix (missed fraud
  10, false decline 1, review 0.5, an assumption) gives band edges 0.05 and 0.5 on the calibrated
  probability; on test the bands hold 0.8687, 0.1150 and 0.0163 of rows at a realised cost of
  0.1492 per row against 0.3480 for approving everything (ADR 0029). Isotonic calibration bought
  nothing measurable: raw Brier 0.02186 against 0.02200 calibrated.
- **Segments.** `ProductCD`, because `addr2` is one value on 0.9901 of train rows (ADR 0030).
- **SHAP.** On 5,000 test rows the 12 C columns take 0.2672 of the summed mean absolute value;
  the 36 missing-block indicators take 0.0016.

## 8. The experiments

Stage 7. `docs/experiments.md`; `reports/leakage_delta.json`, `reports/label_latency.json`;
ADRs 0031 and 0032. The README carries both tables.

The leakage variants switch the train-only guard off by name inside a context manager that
restores it, and the published aggregation (76 columns) lives in the script and nowhere under
`src/`. The post-processing diagnostic explains the deflation: of the test window's 3,083 fraud
rows, 585 sit on singleton entities, 1,408 on all-fraud multi-row entities and 1,090 on mixed
ones, and averaging scores over an entity pulls the mixed ones toward their legitimate
neighbours. The random test rows share an entity with the random train rows 0.7410 of the time
against 0.3402 chronologically.

The label latency is a deterministic N-day cutoff, all or nothing inside the window; the target
encoder is refitted on the labels the model sees, and the refit reproduces the cached encodings
to 0.0. The `regime` block per fit records the mean score on the older training rows, the recent
ones and the later windows: at N=60 the immature model scores the older rows at 0.0307 and the
recent ones at 0.0005 in sample.

## 9. Serving

Stage 8. `docs/serving.md`; `reports/parity.json`, `reports/serving_bundle.json`,
`reports/latency.json`, `loadtest/results.json`; ADRs 0033 to 0035.

**The store.** Five grains (entity, card, content, `addr1` node, device node), two-level state
per key (settled, and pending at the latest timestamp) so that same-timestamp ties read the same
as in the batch build, a bounded ring per windowed grain, Welford in the batch path's order.
`get_features` then `commit`, never one call; commits go under `WATCH` on Redis. The feature
definitions are two pure functions both backends share (ADRs 0033, 0034).

**Parity.** The first 30 days of the file, 134,339 rows, 70,221 entities, 8,641 cards, 40
features: largest absolute difference 0.0 on the in-process backend and 0.0 on Redis, null
masks identical, with 3 ties on the entity key, 32 on the card, 278 on the `addr1` node and 2,382
on the device node in the stream. A one-token break of either side (window bound inclusive on
the online side, `side="right"` on the batch side) fails the test.

**The bundle.** The stage 3 pipeline refitted on train, the booster, the calibrator as
breakpoints, the band edges and the schema, registered as `models:/fraud-detector@production` in
a SQLite registry. 500 test rows through the serving path against batch scoring: 0.0; the
calibrator against scikit-learn on validation: 0.0; band edges as raw scores against stage 6:
0.0.

**Latency.** Medians of five fresh processes' p50 on 300 test rows, milliseconds: raw frame 4.60,
preparation 23.8, schema 3.41, matrix 2.08, booster 0.081, calibrate and band 0.007, five SHAP
factors 1.81; whole path without the store 37.6. Store reads 0.041 in-process and 0.183 on
Redis. The booster is pinned to one thread: pinned single-row p50 0.077 to 0.081 against 0.119
to 0.123 unpinned; the batch of 1,000 goes the other way (ADR 0035).

**Load.** 2,000 test rows in stream order with per-key holds, Redis store, client on the same
ten cores. One worker peaks at 25.2 requests per second at one client; four workers peak at 62.1
at eight clients. Zero errors after the keep-alive was raised for the load test.

## 10. Monitoring and the lifecycle

Stage 9. `docs/monitoring.md`; `reports/monitoring/drift.json`, `reports/monitoring/decay.json`,
`reports/adversarial_validation.json`, `reports/lifecycle_demo.json`; ADRs 0036 to 0039.

**Drift job.** Decile histograms fitted on train for the model's 186 columns and its score. Ten
weekly in-control batches across val and test (8,111 to 21,273 rows) calibrate per column the
larger of 0.20 and the worst in-control value, strictly above; the score edge the same way; a
count edge from leaving each batch out. The 16 target encodings are reported and never alert,
because their values move with the cumulative count by construction (edges 0.21 to 11.26). Held
out one week at a time: the textbook 0.20 band alerts on 16 to 20 columns every week (mean
18.4); the calibrated rule flags no week (ADRs 0036, 0037).

**Decay job.** Refuses a batch before its labels have matured (30 days, an input). Per matured
batch: precision, recall, PR-AUC with a row bootstrap, and the drop against the reference's
draws as an independent difference. The reference reproduces stage 6 to 0.0. Largest in-control
drop 0.0510 against a tolerance of 0.06; four drops have intervals excluding zero and none
clears the tolerance.

**What the drift is.** Adversarial validation, LightGBM over five folds, train against test on
the shipped stack: AUC 1.0000, with `addr2_te` at 0.6949 of the gain and the target encodings at
0.9815 as a group. Without them, 0.9031, led by `id_13` and `D11`, stage 2's top drifters.
Without identifiers, encodings and timedeltas, 0.8600. The drift on this file is population
turnover and the calendar, not concept drift.

**The gate.** `PROMOTION_MARGIN = 0.06`, the widest paired half-width by card (0.0592) rounded
up at the second decimal; `DECAY_TOLERANCE` equal to it; `SUSTAINED_CYCLES = 2`. A challenger
is fitted on a trailing 60-day window under a moved training boundary, calibrated on the next 14
days, and gated on the 14 after that by a paired bootstrap by entity: promoted when the point
clears the margin and the lower bound is above zero (ADR 0038). The registry keeps `previous`
on promotion and rollback restores it.

**The demo.** Val and test as nine weekly batches; from batch 3 the amount is multiplied by 100
and every C column set to 3c + 7. Fourteen cycles, four challengers, two promotions (+0.0954,
+0.0621), two refusals (-0.0438 not better, +0.0424 under the margin), one rollback drill with a
served-path difference of 0.0. No challenger saw a drifted row: the maturity, calibration and
gate windows consumed every matured drifted row on a 62-day stream. The page under `docs/demo/`
is a static build of the artifact (ADR 0039).
